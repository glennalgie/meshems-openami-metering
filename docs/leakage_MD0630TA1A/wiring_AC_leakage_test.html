MD0630 — AC leakage bench test

Injecting a known AC residual current with an isolated 9 V AC source, to read the AC channel and cross the 30 mA AC threshold.

Companion to the DC resistor-ladder test
AC injection (isolated 9 V) +12 V power UART / Modbus GND (common)

Expected reading vs turns

Turns through CTCT-sensed AC (≈9 mA/turn)vs 30 mA threshold
2 turns~18 mAbelow → no alarm
3 turns~27 mAbelow → no alarm
4 turns~36 mAabove → ALARM
5 turns~45 mAabove → alarm

A light-loaded AC adapter often outputs a bit above 9 V, so real mA/turn may be ~9–12. Trust the module’s live reading — wind turns until it crosses 30 mA.

Procedure

  1. Keep the readout (zone B) wired as in the DC test; flash the real-read firmware (-DLEAKAGE_BRINGUP, MOCK off).
  2. Build the AC loop (zone A): 9 V AC → 1 kΩ → wire through the CT → back. Start with 2 turns.
  3. Power on, capture serial: the AC channel now reads >0 while DC stays ~0 (this also re-confirms 0x0001 = AC).
  4. Add turns to raise the reading: aim ~27 mA (no alarm), then ~36 mA (alarm) to show the 30 mA crossing.

Safety

230 V mains stays inside the adapter. Never open or probe the mains side. The Mascot 8310 is an isolating transformer, so its 9 V AC output is SELV (safe extra-low voltage) and galvanically separated from the mains.

• Never short the 9 V output without the series resistor (it would pull up to 340 mA).
• A 1 kΩ ¼ W resistor is ample (dissipation ~0.08 W at 9 mA).
• The AC loop needs no electrical connection to the ESP32/module — it only threads the CT window.
